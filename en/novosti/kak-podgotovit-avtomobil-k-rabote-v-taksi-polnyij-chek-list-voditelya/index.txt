12:T1e91,<figure class="rimg" data-reveal><img src="https://car-city.pro/themes/img/kopiya-2-(2).png" alt="How to prepare a car for taxi work: a full driver&#39;s checklist" loading="lazy" decoding="async" onerror="this.parentNode.classList.add('noimg')"/></figure>
<p>Taxi work doesn&#39;t start with the first order, it starts with getting the car ready. Before going on the line you need to check your documents and the car&#39;s technical condition, tidy up the interior and make sure everything you need for the shift is at hand. Preparing the car properly helps you avoid unpleasant surprises during trips and makes things comfortable for both the driver and the passengers.</p>
<h2 id="what-requirements-a-taxi-car-must-meet">What requirements a taxi car must meet</h2><p>The first thing to figure out is what a taxi car needs from the point of view of the law and of the service through which the driver plans to get orders.</p>
<p>The car must be entered in the regional register of passenger taxis. The carrier must also have the right to do this work, confirmed by an entry in the regional register of carriers. Specific requirements for the car&#39;s appearance may differ from region to region.</p>
<p>Aggregators set their own requirements too. For example, when deciding which fare classes are available, Yandex Taxi looks at the car&#39;s model, year and average market value. The final decision on allowing a specific car into a fare class stays with the service.</p>
<h2 id="checking-the-technical-condition-before-going-on-the-line">Checking the technical condition before going on the line</h2><p>Even a car in perfect working order should be checked before every shift. This is especially important with heavy use: in a day a taxi can spend much more time on the road than an ordinary personal car.</p>
<p>Before you leave, pay attention to these things:</p>
<ul class="tiles">
<li>tyre condition and pressure;</li>
<li>headlights, side lights, brake lights and indicators working;</li>
<li>fuel level or the electric car&#39;s battery charge;</li>
<li>no fault warnings on the dashboard;</li>
<li>wipers working and windows in good condition;</li>
<li>fluid levels;</li>
<li>the climate system working;</li>
<li>no strange noises or other signs of a possible fault.</li>
</ul>
<p>If the car behaves unusually, it&#39;s better to find the cause before your shift starts. Putting off diagnostics until the fault stops you from completing an order is a poor way to save time.</p>
<p>A simple habit is useful for a new driver: do this check in the same order every time. After a while it takes just a few minutes, and the chance of missing something becomes much lower.</p>
<h2 id="getting-the-body-and-interior-ready">Getting the body and interior ready</h2><p>The next step is how the car looks. Here it&#39;s important to look at the car as a passenger would: things the driver stops noticing in daily use may catch the eye of someone who has just got in.</p>
<p>Before a shift, it&#39;s best to wash the body if it&#39;s noticeably dirty and clean the windows and lights. Pay special attention to door handles and sills: they get dirty fast in rain and snow.</p>
<p>Inside, remove rubbish, wipe the surfaces and mats, and check that the seats are clean. It&#39;s better not to use strong air fresheners: a smell the driver likes may be unpleasant for a passenger.</p>
<p>Don&#39;t turn the boot into a permanent storage place for personal things. A passenger may have suitcases, a pram or other large luggage, so it&#39;s better to keep the space free in advance.</p>
<p>For Yandex Taxi drivers the car&#39;s condition matters even more because of photo checks. The service may ask for photos of the body, the interior and certain parts of the car, so keep the car in order all the time, not only right before a check.</p>
<h2 id="what-a-driver-should-have-in-the-car">What a driver should have in the car</h2><p>To get a car ready for taxi work, gather in advance the things you may need during the shift. Besides the required documents and the equipment the rules require, these will come in handy:</p>
<ul class="tiles">
<li>a car charger and a reliable cable for your smartphone;</li>
<li>a phone holder;</li>
<li>wipes and small products for a quick clean;</li>
<li>spare windscreen washer fluid in the right season;</li>
<li>drinking water;</li>
<li>the personal things you need for the shift.</li>
</ul>
<p>One simple life hack for new drivers is to keep your work things in fixed places.</p>
<p>Before a long shift, also fill up or charge the car in advance. Stopping at a petrol or charging station when demand is high means you may miss orders.</p>
<h2 id="getting-your-smartphone-and-work-app-ready">Getting your smartphone and work app ready</h2><p>For a taxi driver, the smartphone is a work tool just like the car. So before a shift, check the battery level, mobile internet, location services and navigation.</p>
<p>If you get orders through an aggregator app, make sure it works properly and you have the latest version installed. This is general advice whatever service you choose.</p>
<p>If you work through Yandex, before going on the line check Yandex Pro: access to your profile, no restrictions and the app being ready to take orders. It&#39;s also worth learning the service&#39;s main features in advance rather than during a trip.</p>
<p>Fix the phone securely so that the driver can see the navigation without holding the device or getting distracted from the road.</p>
<h2 id="when-it-s-best-to-go-on-the-line">When it's best to go on the line</h2><p>There is no single time that guarantees high earnings. Demand depends on the city, the day of the week, the area, the weather, events and many other factors.</p>
<p>Usually the number of trips grows at the hours when lots of people go to work or come back home.  Extra demand can come on Friday evenings, at weekends, during big concerts and sports events, in bad weather or when public transport has problems.</p>
<p>It&#39;s more useful for a driver to follow demand in the app rather than a fixed rule like &quot;always leave at exactly seven in the morning&quot;, and to gradually work out which hours and areas pay best for their own schedule.</p>
<p>One more thing: a high trip price on its own doesn&#39;t mean the shift will be more profitable. Take into account the pick-up time, the distance to the passenger, traffic jams, fuel consumption and the chance of getting the next order after the trip ends.</p>
<h2 id="checklist-before-going-on-the-line">Checklist before going on the line</h2><p>Even an experienced driver finds it easier to have a short check routine:</p>
<ul class="tiles">
<li>documents are in order and the car is approved for work;</li>
<li>there is enough fuel or charge to start the shift;</li>
<li>tyres, lights and wipers are working;</li>
<li>there are no fault warnings on the dashboard;</li>
<li>the body and windows are clean;</li>
<li>there is no rubbish or odd smells in the interior;</li>
<li>the boot is ready for the passenger&#39;s things;</li>
<li>the smartphone is charged, the internet and location services work;</li>
<li>the charger and phone holder are in place;</li>
<li>the work app is ready to take orders.</li>
</ul>
<p>These tips for new drivers quickly turn into a habit. The main thing is not to treat the preparation as a formality: a few minutes before a shift can save you much more time on the line.</p>
<h2 id="a-car-for-taxi-work">A car for taxi work</h2><p>If you don&#39;t have a suitable car of your own, you can rent one from Car City. The fleet has cars of different classes, and the managers help with documents, connecting to aggregators and other questions before you go on the line. All the driver has to do is choose a car and get ready for the first shift.</p>
