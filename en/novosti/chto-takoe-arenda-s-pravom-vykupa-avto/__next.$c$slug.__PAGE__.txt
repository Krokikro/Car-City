<p>You&#39;ve surely seen the phrase &quot;rent-to-own&quot;. It sounds like something between an instalment plan and a rental. In practice it is a separate financial tool with its own rules. And for many drivers, especially those earning in a taxi, it turns out to be the only real way to get a car.</p>
<p>In this article we&#39;ll look at what car rent-to-own is, how it works, and how it differs from a loan, leasing and classic rental. We&#39;ll also talk separately about how buying out a taxi car works, because this is the niche where the scheme is especially popular.</p>
<h2 id="what-is-renting-a-car-with-a-later-buyout">What is renting a car with a later buyout</h2><p>Let&#39;s start with the basics. Renting a car with a later buyout is a format where you take a car to use, make monthly or daily payments, and part of each payment counts towards the price of the car. Once the total covers the agreed price, the car becomes yours. Neither a bank nor a leasing company takes part in this scheme. You sign a contract directly with the car&#39;s owner or with a company that offers this service.</p>
<p>What the contract fixes: the final price of the car, which doesn&#39;t change during the whole term; the payment amount and its structure; the term after which ownership passes to you; early buyout terms, that is, whether you can pay it off earlier and on what terms; and the conditions under which the car can be taken back.</p>
<h2 id="why-a-car-loan-loses-in-a-fair-comparison">Why a car loan loses in a fair comparison</h2><p>A car loan is the first thing that comes to mind when you need a car but don&#39;t have the full price. But let&#39;s look at it more closely:</p>
<p>The bank will ask for proof of income. Official proof. A 2-NDFL income certificate or a salary account statement. For a taxi driver who works for themselves and gets paid to a card by an aggregator, that&#39;s already a problem.</p>
<p>Next comes your credit history. One late payment three years ago, a microloan, a forgotten mobile phone debt, and the scoring system says no.</p>
<p>Say the loan does get approved. Here&#39;s what awaits you:</p>
<ul class="tiles">
<li>Down payment: 10–20 % of the price. For a car costing 1,5 million that&#39;s 150–300 thousand rubles. Just to get started.</li>
<li>Mandatory KASKO insurance: 60–120 thousand rubles every year, at your own expense. Without it the bank won&#39;t give you the loan.</li>
<li>Interest rate: from 18 % a year and up. Over three years you may overpay up to 40–60 % of the car&#39;s price.</li>
<li>Paperwork takes 5–14 working days. All that time you&#39;re not working and not earning.</li>
</ul>
<p>With rent-to-own there&#39;s none of this. There is no down payment, or it is 2–5 % of the car&#39;s price. No credit history check. The paperwork takes a few hours, and as a rule the driver drives away the same day.</p>
<h2 id="why-leasing-is-not-an-option-for-most-drivers">Why leasing is not an option for most drivers</h2><p>In how it works, leasing is closest to rent-to-own. But in practice it creates barriers that an ordinary driver often can&#39;t get past.</p>
<ul class="ticks">
<li>Focus on business. Leasing programmes in Russia are still built for companies and sole proprietors (IP). A private person can get a lease, but the choice of programmes will be small and the terms less flexible.</li>
<li>Unclear final price. A leasing payment includes depreciation, taxes, insurance and the company&#39;s own fee. Working out how much you actually pay for the car and how much for &quot;thin air&quot; can be hard even with a calculator.</li>
<li>Buyout payment at the end. Many leasing contracts include a separate buyout payment at the end of the term. You paid on time for three years, and at the end they bill you another 200–300 thousand. Surprise.</li>
<li>Down payment. In leasing it is from 10 to 49 % of the car&#39;s price. Even at the lower end, that&#39;s a serious sum.</li>
</ul>
<p>Rent-to-own is straightforward: a fixed price and a clear schedule. You know exactly how much you&#39;ll pay and when the car becomes yours. For someone who just wants to work and earn, not dig into leasing schemes, the difference is fundamental.</p>
<h2 id="how-it-differs-from-regular-rental">How it differs from regular rental</h2><p>The difference here is obvious, but it&#39;s worth spelling out because people mix them up all the time. With regular rental you pay only for using the car. You drive for a month and return the car. The money is spent, and you have no asset.</p>
<p>With rent-to-own every payment works for you: part is the fee for use, part builds up towards the price. After the agreed term the car becomes yours. If you need a car for a couple of weeks, regular rental is better. If you need it for a year or longer, rental without buyout stops making financial sense. You&#39;ll pay a similar amount but end up with nothing. For example, right now at the Car City taxi fleet, rent-to-own payments for most cars are the same as for regular rental, and sometimes even lower.</p>
<blockquote>
<p>According to our internal statistics, as of March 2026 our drivers take on average 18 months to buy out their car</p>
<p>— Nikita Mironov, head of the rent-to-own department</p>
</blockquote>
<h2 id="who-is-rent-to-own-for-taxi-work-right-for">Who is rent-to-own for taxi work right for?</h2><ul class="ticks">
<li>You don&#39;t have much starting capital to buy a car, but you&#39;re ready to work and pay steadily.</li>
<li>The bank turned down your car loan because of your credit history, no official employment or not enough proven income.</li>
<li>You need to start working fast: not in two weeks, but in two days.</li>
<li>You plan to work in a taxi for a year or longer. If you&#39;re thinking of a couple of months, regular rental will be cheaper. Over the long run, rent-to-own pays off more.</li>
</ul>
<p>More than 3 000 drivers already work with Car City on the rent-to-own programme. We&#39;ve been on the market for over 8 years and in that time we&#39;ve learned to make the process as clear and convenient as possible. Every driver gets paid holiday, 7 days every six months: we understand that a person behind the wheel needs rest to work well and safely. If you want to know the terms, leave a request. We&#39;ll pick a car, work out a comfortable payment schedule and help you start working in the next few days.</p>
