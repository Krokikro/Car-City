16:T2bbe,<figure class="rimg" data-reveal><img src="https://car-city.pro/themes/imgs/card/foto-1.jpg" alt="TOP 10 mistakes drivers make when renting a taxi car" loading="lazy" decoding="async" onerror="this.parentNode.classList.add('noimg')"/></figure>
<p>We go through the 10 most common mistakes when renting a car for taxi work: the contract, hidden payments, choosing a car, documents, costs and tips that will help you avoid financial losses</p>
<p>Is it worth working in a taxi on a rented car? Almost everyone just entering the profession asks themselves this. The answer depends not on the format itself but on how carefully you approach its terms. This is exactly where most new drivers lose money: they take a car without understanding the details, and end up earning less than expected or going into the red in the very first month. Here are the ten mistakes that happen most often and how to avoid them.</p>
<h2 id="mistake-1-not-reading-the-contract-before-signing">Mistake 1. Not reading the contract before signing</h2><p>The very first thing to know when renting a car is the contract terms. Many drivers skim it, nod and sign. Then it turns out there&#39;s a penalty for returning the car early, working with several aggregators is forbidden, and the driver pays for car washes and parking.</p>
<p>Read every clause. If something isn&#39;t clear, ask before signing. Pay special attention to: how penalties are calculated, liability in an accident, and the terms for ending the contract.</p>
<h2 id="mistake-2-not-inspecting-the-car-at-pickup">Mistake 2. Not inspecting the car at pickup</h2><p>When you take a car, record every bit of damage on video: scratches, chips, dents on the wheels, the condition of the sills and mirrors. Without a video record you risk paying for damage that was there before you. The company has the right to bill you for any defect not shown in the handover certificate.</p>
<p>Make it a rule: a certificate with a detailed description of the body and interior is a required document that both sides sign when you get the car.</p>
<h2 id="mistake-3-choosing-a-car-class-without-working-out-the-real-">Mistake 3. Choosing a car class without working out the real profit</h2><p>How do you earn in a taxi on a rented car? Start with simple maths. Drivers often take a Comfort class car counting on high fares but don&#39;t work out the result. Comfort gives more expensive orders, but costs more to rent and gets fewer requests because of its narrower audience. Economy is cheaper to rent and gives a bigger flow of orders.</p>
<p>In Moscow the fare difference between Economy and Comfort for one trip is 30–40 % on average, while the Comfort class rental rate is 500–800 rubles a day higher. Before taking a particular car, do the maths yourself: how many hours a day you&#39;re ready to work, what the average fare is on your routes, and whether your daily earnings cover the daily rate after the aggregator&#39;s commission. Count your total profit per day, not the price of one trip.</p>
<h2 id="mistake-4-ignoring-the-aggregator-s-commission-when-working-">Mistake 4. Ignoring the aggregator's commission when working out earnings</h2><p>A new taxi driver often thinks: &quot;Today I did orders worth 6 000 rubles, so that&#39;s what I earned.&quot; But the aggregator&#39;s commission is 20 to 30 % depending on the fare class and the taxi fleet&#39;s terms, plus rent and fuel. Your final net earnings per shift turn out much lower.</p>
<p>Work out the full cost structure before your first working day: rent + fuel + aggregator commission = the cost of a shift. Only then is it clear how many trips you need to make to come out ahead.</p>
<h2 id="mistake-5-not-asking-who-pays-for-maintenance-and-repairs">Mistake 5. Not asking who pays for maintenance and repairs</h2><p>At some taxi fleets maintenance is included in the rent. At others the driver pays for oil, filter and consumables changes. If the driver causes an accident, the repairs are usually at their expense. Understand in advance what the insurance covers, whether there&#39;s KASKO and how big the excess is.</p>
<p>Ask directly what happens if the car breaks down through no fault of yours: do they give you a replacement while it&#39;s being repaired? Downtime without a car means a direct loss of income. A taxi fleet that takes care of maintenance and technical support and provides a replacement car cuts unexpected costs and doesn&#39;t leave the driver alone with a breakdown.</p>
<h2 id="mistake-6-working-without-a-taxi-permit">Mistake 6. Working without a taxi permit</h2><p>The permit is a required document; without it you&#39;re not allowed to take orders. In the industry it&#39;s often called a taxi driver&#39;s licence, although officially it&#39;s a permit to carry passengers. The fine for not having it under Art. 14.1 of the Code of Administrative Offences (KoAP): for a private person or self-employed driver, 2 000–2 500 rubles with possible confiscation of the car; for a sole proprietor (IP), 100 000 rubles; for a company, 400 000 rubles (Art. 14.1.2 KoAP). For a repeat offence, the vehicle is confiscated.</p>
<p>Documents you need to work in a taxi on a rented car: a driver&#39;s licence with at least 3 years of experience, a passport, a taxi permit, a certificate of no criminal record, registration in KIS &quot;ART&quot; (the system has been mandatory in Moscow and the Moscow region since 2021) and certification on knowledge of the city, mandatory for drivers in Moscow and the Moscow region since March 2025. A reliable company helps you get the documents or provides them as part of the package when you sign the contract.</p>
<h2 id="mistake-7-renting-a-car-from-private-individuals">Mistake 7. Renting a car from private individuals</h2><p>Renting a car from a private person may look cheaper, but it carries serious risks. The insurance often doesn&#39;t cover commercial use, the contract doesn&#39;t protect the driver&#39;s interests, and the aggregator may block the account because the car doesn&#39;t meet the requirements.</p>
<p>How do you rent a car for taxi work the right way? Only through an official taxi fleet or an authorised aggregator partner. This gives you legal protection, predictable terms and support in case of force majeure.</p>
<h2 id="mistake-8-not-checking-the-taxi-fleet-s-reputation">Mistake 8. Not checking the taxi fleet's reputation</h2><p>There are many taxi fleets, and they all have different terms. Some inflate the promised income in their ads, others hide extra fees or set strict requirements for the number of shifts a week. Some take a deposit that&#39;s hard to get back when the contract ends.</p>
<p>Before choosing a company, read reviews from current drivers on independent platforms: Yandex Maps, 2GIS, topic forums. Find out the fleet&#39;s real commission: not the one in the ad, but the one written in the contract. Make sure the car is handed over on the day you apply, without a long wait. A personal chat with someone who already works at this taxi fleet tells you more than any ad.</p>
<h2 id="mistake-9-expecting-maximum-income-from-day-one">Mistake 9. Expecting maximum income from day one</h2><p>How to work in a taxi on a rented car effectively is a question not only of skill but also of time. The first 1–2 weeks go on getting used to things: the aggregator&#39;s interface, the busy order zones and the city&#39;s peak hours. During this period income is below average.</p>
<p>Drivers who expect maximum earnings from day one get disappointed and leave before they&#39;ve figured things out. Plan a month ahead: the first week is for getting used to the work, the second for building your rating, and from the third on you get a steady flow of orders at peak hours.</p>
<h2 id="mistake-10-not-considering-rent-to-own">Mistake 10. Not considering rent-to-own</h2><p>Many drivers pay for car rental for years without getting any closer to owning an asset. Meanwhile, most big taxi fleets offer rent-to-own: part of the daily payments goes towards the price of the car. If you plan to work in a taxi for more than a year, rent-to-own is better than plain rental.</p>
<p>Check the terms in advance: the minimum term, the final buyout price and the paperwork. This fundamentally changes the financial sense of working on a rented car.</p>
<p>Most of these mistakes are made not out of incompetence but out of haste: you want to start earning faster, so the details are left &quot;for later&quot;. The right start in a taxi always means preparation: study the contract, count the real costs, choose a reliable taxi fleet. Then renting a car becomes a work tool, not a source of unexpected costs.</p>
<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2><h3>Do I need to register as a sole proprietor (IP) or self-employed to work on a rented car?</h3><p>Yes, without a tax status you can&#39;t work legally. Most taxi fleets require self-employed or IP status: it&#39;s a must for registering in KIS &quot;ART&quot; and getting a taxi permit. Self-employment is easier to set up: registration through the &quot;Moy Nalog&quot; (My Tax) app takes a few minutes, and the tax rate is 4% on income from private individuals.</p>
<h3>Can I work with several aggregators at the same time?</h3><p>It depends on the terms of your contract with the taxi fleet. With standard rental the car is tied to a specific fleet and aggregator, and working elsewhere is forbidden and may lead to the contract being ended. With rent-to-own the limits on choosing an aggregator are usually lifted: check this clause before signing.</p>
<h3>What should I do after an accident in a rented car?</h3><p>The first step is to tell the taxi fleet: most big companies have round-the-clock technical support and a clear procedure for this case. At the same time, fill in a European accident report or call the traffic police (GIBDD). Don&#39;t do repairs yourself or make deals on the spot without telling the taxi fleet: this breaks the contract terms and may cost you the insurance payout.</p>
<h3>What is the usual minimum rental term at taxi fleets?</h3><p>Most taxi fleets have a minimum rental term of 30 days. Short-term rental for a few days is rare and usually costs much more per day. If you want to try taxi work before deciding, ask about a trial period: some companies offer the first day free.</p>
<h3>Does the class of the rented car affect the number of orders?</h3><p>Yes, a lot. Economy class has the biggest flow of orders because of mass demand, but a low average fare. Comfort and above mean fewer orders, but each costs more. For a new driver Economy is more practical: you build your rating faster and it&#39;s easier to understand how the aggregator works with a dense flow of requests.</p>
