Which cars are most often used as taxis

Contents

  1. Requirements for a taxi car
  2. Top 5 cars proven by taxi fleets
  3. New or used?

It feels like all the taxis on the city streets look alike. That's no accident: over time drivers and taxi fleets have built up their own list of cars that handle the pace of work well. And while some brands are slowly leaving the market, others are only getting stronger.

We've put together an overview of which cars are most often chosen for taxi work and why they became the "workhorses" of the industry.

Requirements for a taxi car

Taxi work means a heavy daily load on the car, so there are a number of requirements for it. Some are set by law, others by the aggregators and taxi fleets themselves.

Year of manufacture

The law doesn't always clearly limit the car's age, but regions and services usually set their own limits. In Moscow and big cities they most often require a car no older than 10 years, in some regions up to 15 years. The logic is simple: the newer the car, the lower the chance of a breakdown and the more comfort for passengers.

Body type and number of doors

Category B passenger cars with four doors are allowed as taxis. This standard makes it easy for passengers to get in and out and lets you carry luggage. Two-door cars or coupes can't be used as taxis.

Colour

The car must be easy to recognise. The chequered taxi stripe is often put on the body, and an orange light on the roof. The body colour depends on the region. In Moscow, for example, there is a requirement for a single yellow colour, but other cities may have no such rules.

Technical condition

The main thing in a taxi car is its overall condition. It goes through technical inspection more often, roughly every six months. For the passenger it also matters how the body looks, whether there's rust or dents, whether the interior is well kept, whether the air conditioning works and the seat belts are in order. These details shape the impression, and the passenger is more likely to leave a good review.

Equipment

Information about the driver and the licence is placed inside the taxi. In some cases a tachograph is also required, but for category B passenger cars this is usually not needed.

Documents

The documents for taxi work are standard: the vehicle registration certificate, an OSAGO policy marked "Taxi", a waybill and a permit to carry passengers, so that the car is entered in the regional register.

But there are some differences depending on who goes on the line.

  • If a sole proprietor (IP) works, they also need IP registration and an extract from the EGRIP register.
  • A self-employed driver only needs to confirm their status through the "Moy Nalog" (My Tax) app.
  • A company, such as a taxi fleet, needs its founding documents and an extract from the EGRUL register.

The driver must have all documents at hand: this is one of the conditions for going on the line. Once the formalities are done and the car is in order, the question of choosing a model comes first.

Top 5 cars proven by taxi fleets

Exeed LX

Exeed LX has gradually found its place in taxi fleets in big cities. This compact crossover feels at home in heavy traffic and still has enough room for passengers. The boot easily fits suitcases or large bags, which is handy for airport work. High ground clearance and a strong suspension help you drive calmly over potholes and speed bumps.

An important advantage of the model is its economy and modern technical "insides". The 1.5 engine with a CVT runs smoothly, without jerks, and the suspension was tuned with Russian conditions in mind. The car doesn't need frequent attention, so regular maintenance costs reasonable money.

Why Exeed LX is chosen for taxi work:

  1. Passengers don't complain about being cramped: the interior is roomy.
  2. Suitcases and bags fit without fighting over space in the boot.
  3. The suspension easily "swallows" potholes and kerbs.
  4. Fuel consumption stays normal even with heavy use.
  5. No extra questions come up at the service centre.

Москвич 3

Москвич 3 has become one of the symbols of the return of local production. The car has a simple design, so service centres and mechanics understand it well. For taxi fleets this is also a plus: the fewer complex solutions, the easier it is to maintain a fleet of several cars. The interior has enough room for three passengers, and the rear seats are designed with frequent trips in mind.

The car is assembled in Moscow, with quick access to spare parts. The model also has an electric version, the Москвич 3е. It was made for the city: charging at the fleet or in car parks lets you save on fuel, and running costs are lower.

Why Москвич 3 is chosen for taxi work:

  1. The car is easy to maintain: any service centre can repair it.
  2. Spare parts are always on sale, so you won't be stuck for weeks.
  3. The interior is made for frequent trips, and passengers are comfortable in the back row.
  4. There is an electric version: you can save on fuel and charge at city stations.
  5. The car costs reasonable money and suits those who get a taxi car on their own.

Haval F7

This year Haval F7 has actively joined corporate fleets. The model is adapted for heavy use. The car is assembled in Tula, so getting spare parts won't be a problem. It's bigger than many competitors, so you can carry passengers in comfort and take big orders, for example airport transfers.

Taxis often use simpler versions of the Haval F7 without extra decorative parts, but with tough materials that are easy to wash. The car also has a high safety rating. It has airbags, a stability system and driver assists for city driving.

Why Haval F7 is chosen for taxi work:

  1. There is enough room inside for passengers to ride in comfort.
  2. The trim is practical: wipe it and it's clean again.
  3. Service centres and spare parts are available all over the country.
  4. Driver assists and safety systems help the driver.
  5. The car is equally comfortable in the city and on the highway.

Kia Rio

Kia Rio is one of the most popular cars in the taxi Economy class. The car is affordable, easy to maintain and still comfortable for passengers. Thanks to its compact size, the Rio handles city traffic well, and its economical engine helps you spend less on fuel.

Drivers value this car for its reliability and simple mechanics: any service centre has the parts, and maintenance doesn't hurt your wallet. The interior is roomy enough for three passengers, and the boot easily fits a couple of suitcases or a pram.

Why Kia Rio is chosen for taxi work:

  1. An economical engine and low maintenance costs.
  2. A compact but comfortable interior for passengers.
  3. The boot suits everyday trips and transfers.
  4. Spare parts and service centres are available in any city.
  5. The car is reliable and suits long work on the line.

Skoda Rapid

Skoda Rapid holds a strong position in city taxi fleets. Drivers note its easy handling and economical fuel consumption. The model has become common on the used car market. Passengers like the roomy back row and smooth ride, so a trip in a Rapid usually leaves a pleasant impression.

From a practical point of view, the car combines comfort and predictable costs well. It doesn't need frequent repairs as long as it's serviced regularly, and all main parts are available in any big city.

Why Rapid is chosen for taxi work:

  1. A big boot and easy loading.
  2. An economical engine means lower fuel costs.
  3. There is always plenty to choose from on the used car market.
  4. A comfortable interior for passengers.

New or used?

Whether to get a new or used car for taxi work isn't only about money. Your expectations from the work matter too. A new car means fewer risks, a warranty and modern technology. But you'll have to accept that in the first years it comes with loan or leasing payments.

A used car is more attractive in price, especially on a limited budget. But along with the savings come extra worries. You need to understand the car's condition, check its history carefully and be ready to invest in it. Sometimes it's better to get a used car in good shape than to overpay for a new one, especially if you plan to work and change cars in a couple of years.

There's also a third way. Car City offers rent-to-own cars for taxi work. The driver doesn't have to make big upfront investments: the car is ready to work right away, and all payments are spread over time. You can work on a fully serviced car and later, if the terms suit you, buy it out on convenient terms.

In the end the choice depends on your approach. For long-term taxi work without risks, people take rental or new cars; for a quick start, used ones. But it's not only the purchase price you need to count. Maintenance, insurance and downtime often matter more than the difference in price.
