How to become a taxi driver in 2026: requirements, documents and registration

A step-by-step guide to becoming a taxi driver in 2026: what documents you need, requirements for the car and the driver, and how to get registered and start working in a taxi.

Taxi work is still one of the easiest ways to earn money, but since 2026 the rules of the game have changed. New requirements for cars have come into force, control over the self-employed has become tighter, and making your work legal has become stricter.

We go through a step-by-step plan for a legal start that takes all the changes into account, so you can go on the line with confidence and avoid unexpected fines or account blocks. The guide is based on current legal requirements and the terms of the largest taxi aggregators.

Requirements for the driver

The law sets clear criteria for people who get behind the wheel of a taxi:

  • Minimum age: 21 (premium fare classes sometimes require 25 or older).
  • Driving experience: at least 3 years of driving a category B car.
  • No criminal record. You need a certificate showing no outstanding convictions for transport-related or serious offences.
  • Citizenship or legal status. Russians can work, as well as citizens of Armenia, Belarus, Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan with a tax number (INN) and a driver's licence in Russian.
  • No serious traffic violations in the past year. Losing your licence or repeat fines can lead to a refusal.
  • Medical checks. The driver must have a pre-trip medical check every day, and a scheduled medical board once every two years. A form 003-V/u certificate is required.

Requirements for the car

On 1 March 2026 the localisation law came into force, changing the rules for allowing vehicles into commercial passenger transport. To get into the register and receive a work permit, the car must be made under a special investment contract or score at least 3200 points in the Ministry of Industry and Trade system.

If the car was entered in the taxi register before 1 March 2026, the new requirements don't apply to it, so it can keep working on the old terms.

Besides localisation, other mandatory requirements apply. For the Economy fare class the car must be no older than 8–12 years, depending on the make.

The car must be fitted with:

  • an orange roof light;
  • the chequered taxi stripes on the sides;
  • an information plate about the carrier inside the car;
  • a fire extinguisher;
  • a first aid kit.

The OSAGO policy must state that the car is used as a taxi, otherwise registration is not possible.

Documents for work

To carry passengers legally, you need to collect a set of documents:

  • Passport (all pages).
  • A category B driver's licence with at least 3 years of confirmed experience.
  • A certificate of no criminal record.
  • A form 003-V/u medical certificate.
  • The vehicle registration certificate (STS).
  • A currently valid technical inspection diagnostic card.
  • A taxi OSAGO policy marked for commercial use.
  • A permit (licence), issued for 5 years.
  • A rental or leasing contract, if you don't own the car.
  • The self-employed will also need a registration certificate from the "Moy Nalog" (My Tax) app.
  • Sole proprietors (IP) provide an extract from the EGRIP register no older than 30 days.

Upload all documents in good quality, because aggregators and government bodies check them carefully.

Registration: self-employed or IP

The tax status you choose affects how much you pay and what you can do.

  • Self-employment (NPD) suits working on your own car with a modest income. The tax rate is 4 % when working with private individuals and 6 % with companies. Registration takes 5 minutes in the "Moy Nalog" app or through Gosuslugi. The income limit is 2,4 million rubles a year. You can't hire workers and you don't build up insurance record for your pension, but there are no reports and no contributions.
  • A sole proprietorship (IP) gives more options: you can hire drivers, use the patent or simplified tax system, and deduct expenses. For taxis, the patent (6 % of potential income) or the simplified system USN 6 % (on income) are popular. But you'll have to pay insurance contributions for yourself and file reports. An IP is opened in 3–5 working days, the fee is 800 rubles.

Aggregators work with both the self-employed and sole proprietors. If you plan to work a lot, an IP is often better because there's no income limit and you can save on contributions.

Getting a permit (licence)

A permit is required for everyone: the self-employed, sole proprietors and companies. Without it the work is illegal, with fines of up to 50 000 rubles for a driver and up to 400 000 rubles for companies.

How to get it:

  1. Apply online, at an MFC public service centre or at the regional transport department.
  2. Attach: passport, STS, inspection card, OSAGO marked "taxi", and a document confirming your tax status.
  3. Pay the state fee (800–1500 rubles in most regions, often free on Gosuslugi).
  4. Wait for the decision (from 5 to 20 working days).
  5. Get the permit and enter the car in the regional taxi register.

The permit is tied to a specific car and issued for 5 years. If you change cars, you have to go through the procedure again.

Connecting to an aggregator

After you get the permit, register with taxi ordering services. The most popular in Russia are Yandex Taxi, Citymobil, Uber, Maxim and Gett.

You can connect directly through the aggregator's app or through a partner taxi fleet. If you connect on your own, you need to fill in a form, upload documents and pass a check. The aggregator's commission is from 16 % to 23 %, depending on the fare class.

If you connect through a partner, the fleet handles the paperwork, gives you a car and sorts out documents. But the driver pays rent (usually 1500–3000 rubles/day) and sometimes an extra commission.

How much you can earn

A taxi driver's income in the capital depends on many factors: hours worked, car class, how you work and even the time of day. It's important to rely on real figures, not on record earnings promised in adverts.

According to the "Avito Podrabotki" service, in December 2025 – January 2026 the average pay offered to part-time taxi drivers in Moscow was about 74 800 rubles a month. That's the second highest in Russia after Saint Petersburg, where the offer was about 92 thousand rubles.

Market players give more promising comments. For example, Mikhail Mikhailov, president of the Association for the Development of the Taxi Industry, said at a press conference that the total income of taxi drivers working in the Moscow region in the economy segment can reach 300 000 rubles.

Experienced drivers warn that you can't predict your income. Earnings depend heavily on the season, weather, day of the week and even the time of day. Many newcomers face a reality where big numbers hide rental costs and commissions, plus tiredness and an unpredictable flow of orders.

Keep track of income and costs from day one. It's the only way to see your real profit and avoid losses. Many drivers use simple spreadsheets or mobile apps to track the financial result of each shift and gradually find the schedule and way of working that pays best for them.

Only careful preparation at the start and a realistic view of your costs will help you avoid disappointment in the first months and turn taxi work into a steady source of income.
