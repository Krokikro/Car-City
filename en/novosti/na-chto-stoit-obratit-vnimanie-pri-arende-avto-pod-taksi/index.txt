<p>Tips on renting a taxi car: how to choose the right car and not overpay. We go through the important details that help drivers avoid problems and make rental pay off.</p>
<h2 id="renting-a-taxi-car-what-matters-when-choosing">Renting a taxi car: what matters when choosing?</h2><p>Renting a car for taxi work is a good alternative to buying your own, especially at the start of your career. You avoid the hassle of paperwork and bureaucracy, maintenance, insurance and even dealing with the insurance company after an accident. When choosing a car to earn money with, keep a few things in mind. Just taking a car &quot;for a drive&quot; and using it for intensive taxi work are two very different things.</p>
<h3>Key points of renting a taxi car:</h3>
<ol class="stepl">
<li><strong>Mileage limit.</strong> One of the important points in a rental contract is the daily mileage limit. 200-250 km, which is enough for personal trips, is not at all enough for a taxi. Taxi work means constantly driving around the city, and going over the limit will mean extra costs. Always ask how much each &quot;extra&quot; kilometre costs and work out how good the offer will be given how much you plan to work. Some companies offer packages with unlimited mileage, but they usually cost more. Weigh all the pros and cons and choose the best option.</li>
<li><strong>Where you can drive.</strong> Check the rental contract carefully for limits on where you can drive. Some companies only allow the car within the city, others allow trips around the region or beyond. This is especially important if you plan to work on a minivan or estate car, which are often ordered for trips out of town, to the airport, to the station or between cities. The limits may be linked to the insurance terms and to the taxi fleet&#39;s own policy.</li>
<li><strong>Insurance (KASKO and OSAGO).</strong> An OSAGO policy is mandatory for using any car. KASKO insures the car against damage and theft. When renting a taxi car, KASKO is recommended, because heavy use in dense city traffic increases the risk of accidents. Find out whether KASKO is included in the rental price or has to be paid separately.</li>
<li><strong>The car&#39;s technical condition.</strong> Before signing the rental contract, always inspect the car for visible damage and check that all systems work (lights, brakes, air conditioning, power windows). Take a test drive to assess the car&#39;s technical condition. Record all existing faults so the owner can&#39;t make claims against you when you return the car.</li>
<li><strong>Payment schedule and terms.</strong> Find out the schedule and ways of paying the rent. Some companies require daily payment, others weekly or monthly. Ask whether there&#39;s a deposit and how it&#39;s returned. At Carcity there are no non-refundable deposits or accumulating deposits, and the rental and payment schedule is flexible.</li>
<li><strong>Car branding.</strong> If you plan to work with a taxi aggregator, find out whether the rented car needs branding and whether the taxi fleet provides it. CarCity gives drivers cars that are already branded.</li>
<li><strong>Extra equipment.</strong> If you need extra equipment for work (a child seat, a navigator, etc.), ask whether you can rent it or have it installed in the car.</li>
</ol>
<p>Studying all the terms of the rental contract carefully and paying attention to details will help you avoid unpleasant surprises and make taxi work comfortable and profitable. Ask the car owner about anything that isn&#39;t clear. The right car and good rental terms are the key to successful taxi work.</p>
